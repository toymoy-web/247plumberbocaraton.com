If a burst pipe, roof leak, or storm has left standing water in your home, water damage restoration is the cleanup and drying work that stops mold and structural damage before they start, and it's a different job from fixing whatever caused the leak.

When It's a Restoration Job, Not a Plumbing Job

Fixing the pipe and drying the house are two different jobs

A burst pipe is a plumbing repair. The wet drywall, soaked flooring, and damp insulation left behind afterward is water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, and mold prevention. Both often get called in the same afternoon, but they're billed and staffed differently, and our 24/7 emergency line for standing water and drying work runs on a dedicated number so restoration calls don't get mixed in with routine plumbing dispatch.

South Florida's combination of hurricane season, sudden downpours, and humidity means water damage from a roof leak or storm surge doesn't dry on its own the way it might in a drier climate. Left wet for more than 24-48 hours, drywall and subfloor start growing mold, which is why the first call after a flood matters more here than in most parts of the country.

24/7 Emergency

Emergency water damage: why the first hours matter

Standing water, a soaked ceiling or wet carpet is an emergency from the first hour. Water spreads into drywall, subfloor and insulation faster than most people expect, and the damage grows while you wait.

Our 24/7 emergency line is open for active leaks, burst pipes and flooding. We stop the water, extract it and start drying so the damage stays as small as possible.

Signs You Need Restoration, Not Just a Repair

What water damage actually looks like a day or two later

Warped or soft flooring

Laminate, hardwood, or vinyl that bubbles or feels spongy underfoot after a leak has already absorbed water into the subfloor.

A musty smell that won't clear

Usually a sign of moisture trapped behind drywall or under flooring, even if the surface looks dry.

Discolored ceiling or wall patches

Yellow-brown staining from a slow roof or plumbing leak that's been running longer than one storm.

How It's Done

Extraction, drying, and a moisture check before we call it done

The process starts with a moisture reading, not a guess, using the same meters that confirm when drywall and subfloor are actually dry rather than just dry to the touch. Standing water gets extracted first, then industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until the moisture readings come back to a normal baseline, which for a typical single-room flood in Boca Raton's humidity usually takes three to five days.

Anything porous that stayed wet more than 48 hours, carpet padding, drywall below the waterline, particle-board cabinet bases, generally needs to come out rather than just dry out, since it can hold moisture and mold spores even after the surface looks fine.

Insurance & Documentation

What to document before cleanup starts

Before we arrive, if it's safe to do so:

  • • Photograph standing water and any visibly damaged flooring, drywall, or belongings.
  • • Note the approximate time the leak or flooding started.
  • • Shut off water at the main if the source is a plumbing failure, not storm water.
  • • Move unaffected furniture and electronics away from the wet area if it's safe to do so.

Most homeowner's policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe or storm, but coverage details vary by policy and by whether the damage is classified as gradual versus sudden, so check your specific policy or ask your adjuster directly rather than assuming. Industry Standard

What a proper drying job actually follows

Water damage restoration in the US generally follows the IICRC S500 standard, a published reference for water damage restoration that sets out how categories of water contamination (clean, gray, or black water) and classes of damage should be assessed and dried. Referencing that standard is a statement about the process, not a claim to a specific certification we hold. Ask directly if that distinction matters for your insurance claim, since some carriers request it by name in the estimate.
