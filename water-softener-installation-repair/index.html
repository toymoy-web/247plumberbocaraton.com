Is Boca Raton's Water Actually Hard?

What's really in the water here

Boca Raton's municipal water comes from the Biscayne Aquifer, a limestone formation, and the city's 2024 Annual Drinking Water Quality Report describes lime softening and membrane softening at the plant. That report does not give a hardness number, so we do not quote one. Test your own tap with a hardness strip, or ask Utility Services at 561-338-7300, before you decide on a softener.

Ask your building department what applies to the brine drain connection for a softener. Florida plumbing rules are based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Some HOAs in Boca Raton's condo and townhome communities also have their own rules about softener discharge, particularly for units connected to a shared drain stack, which is worth checking with your association before installation rather than after.

A pre-installation water test also lets us confirm whether iron or other minerals beyond calcium and magnesium are present, since a standard softener resin isn't always the right match for every mineral profile. Homes on older galvanized supply lines can also show iron staining that a standard softener won't fully resolve on its own, which is worth flagging during the estimate rather than after installation.

Before You Call

What to check first

  • • Check the brine tank salt level. A system low on salt won't regenerate properly.
  • • Note how old the unit is and whether it's been serviced before.
  • • If water tastes salty, mention that specifically, since it points to a different issue than general hardness.

If you're renting or in a condo, check whether a softener is already installed on the unit before assuming you need a new one. Some buildings have a shared or building-level system that individual units tap into.

Sizing

Why grain capacity matters more than brand

A softener's grain capacity, how much hardness it can remove before needing to regenerate, should be matched to your household's actual daily water use and your water's specific hardness level, not just picked based on square footage or bedroom count. An undersized system regenerates too frequently and wears out faster. An oversized one wastes salt and water on regeneration cycles that run more often than necessary.

We test your specific water's hardness level as part of the estimate rather than assuming a standard number for the area, since even homes on the same street can see some variation depending on their particular connection to the municipal supply.
