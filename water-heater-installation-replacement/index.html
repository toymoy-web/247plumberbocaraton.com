Choosing a Unit

Tank, tankless, or heat pump

Tank

The most familiar option and usually the lowest upfront cost, sized by gallon capacity to household size.

Tankless

Heats water on demand rather than storing it, which saves space and can lower running costs for households with steady hot water use.

Heat pump

Pulls heat from surrounding air rather than generating it directly, which fits Florida's warm climate well and can lower electricity use over a standard electric tank.

Sizing It Right

Why household size actually matters

A tank sized for a two-person household will run out of hot water fast in a family of five, and an oversized tank in a small condo wastes energy keeping water hot that never gets used. Capacity should match your household's simultaneous hot water use, back-to-back showers, a dishwasher running at the same time, not just square footage.

For tankless units, sizing works differently: instead of a gallon capacity, the unit is rated by flow rate, how many gallons per minute it can heat on demand. A household that regularly runs a shower and the washing machine at the same time needs a higher flow-rate unit than one that rarely doubles up on hot water use. We walk through your actual household patterns before recommending a specific model, rather than defaulting to a mid-range size.

What to Expect

The day of installation

Most tank replacements are a same-day job: the old unit is drained and removed, the new one is set, connected, and tested, and any required permit inspection is scheduled separately. A tankless conversion or a fuel-type change (electric to gas or the reverse) usually takes longer, since it can involve new gas piping, electrical work, or venting that a straight tank swap doesn't need.

Before Installation Day

What to do before we arrive

  • • Clear the area around the current unit so it can be drained and removed without obstruction.
  • • If switching fuel types, confirm with us in advance whether new gas or electrical work needs its own separate permit.
  • • Know the location of your main water shutoff in case it's needed during the swap.
Permits

When a permit is required

The City of Boca Raton Development Services Department publishes a Water Heater Changeout permit checklist, and it says contractors must be registered and up to date to pull permits. Switching fuel types, say from electric to gas, or from a tank to tankless, can change the scope of work, so ask which permits apply. Development Services answers questions at (561) 393-7930. We go over the permit questions as part of the installation quote rather than as a surprise add-on.

Code & Compliance

What to ask the building department

Ask the building department what its inspection covers for your unit, including the relief valve discharge, strapping and venting. These are safety items, not just paperwork, because a relief valve without a discharge pipe or a poorly vented gas unit is a genuine safety issue.
