Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Boca Raton, FL

A warm patch on the floor, a hissing sound from under the concrete, or water pooling near the foundation with no obvious source are the usual first signs of a slab leak, a leak in the water line running underneath your home's concrete foundation.

Why This Happens Here

Slab-on-grade homes and aging copper lines

Most South Florida homes, Boca Raton included, are built slab-on-grade with the water supply lines run directly through or under the concrete rather than in a basement or crawlspace. In older homes with original copper supply lines, decades of soil contact and water chemistry can corrode the pipe from the outside. Once a pinhole opens, water pressure pushes it out under the slab, sometimes for weeks before it surfaces as a warm spot or a wet patch on the floor.

Hot water lines fail more often than cold ones, since the heat cycling accelerates corrosion in copper pipe over time. That's part of why a warm spot, rather than a cold or damp one, is usually the first sign homeowners notice, often before the water bill shows any real increase.

Before You Call

What to check first

  • • Note exactly where the warm spot or sound is, and whether it's near a hot or cold fixture line.
  • • Check your last two water bills for a jump in usage with no change in habits.
  • • Avoid drilling into or cutting the slab yourself before the leak is professionally located.
Signs

What to look for

A warm spot on the floor

If the leak is on the hot water line, the concrete above it will feel noticeably warmer than the surrounding floor.

A rising water bill

A slab leak can run continuously, which shows up as an unexplained jump in usage before any visible water appears.

The sound of running water

Some slab leaks are audible as a faint hissing or trickling sound, especially at night when the house is quiet.

Repair Options

Spot repair vs. rerouting the line

Once leak detection confirms the exact location, there are generally two honest paths. A spot repair opens a small section of slab to fix that specific point; rerouting sends the line entirely above the slab, through the attic or an exterior wall, so it never runs under concrete again. A spot repair costs less upfront but doesn't address other sections of aging pipe; rerouting costs more but avoids repeat slab leaks from the same line later on.

Code Consideration

Why permits matter for slab work

Opening a section of slab to repair or reroute a water line generally requires a permit under Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), since it affects the structural concrete and the potable water system. Skipping the permit on a slab repair can create a real problem later at resale, when an unpermitted repair shows up during a home inspection and has to be corrected or disclosed. We pull the permit as part of the job rather than treating it as optional.
