Sewer Camera Inspection: What It Actually Shows

By the Boca Raton 24/7 Plumbing team · September 17, 2026

You're either buying an older home and want to know what's really going on with the sewer line before you close, or you've had a recurring drain problem and someone suggested a camera inspection as the next step. Either way, it helps to know what actually happens during one of these and what the footage actually reveals, since "we ran a camera through it" can mean very different things depending on the context.

The basic setup

A sewer camera inspection uses a waterproof camera mounted on the end of a flexible, semi-rigid cable, usually fed into the line through an existing cleanout or, if none exists, through a fixture drain or a small access point created for the purpose. As the cable is pushed and pulled through the pipe, the camera transmits live video to a monitor the technician is watching, and it's usually recorded for later reference. Many camera systems also include a locating transmitter, letting the technician mark the exact position of the camera head from above ground, which is what lets a repair crew later dig or line only the specific section that needs attention.

What the footage actually reveals

Root intrusion

Tree roots entering through cracked joints, visible as hair-like or thicker root masses growing into the pipe interior.

Cracks and breaks

Structural damage to the pipe wall itself, ranging from hairline cracks to full breaks or collapsed sections.

Bellied sections

A section of pipe that's sunk over time, creating a low point that holds standing water even when nothing is actively clogging it.

Offset or separated joints

Sections of pipe that have shifted apart at a joint, often letting soil, roots, or groundwater into the line.

Grease and scale buildup

Material coating the interior walls that restricts flow without being a structural problem, distinguishable from a physical defect.

Pipe material and general condition

Confirmation of whether a line is cast iron, clay, PVC, or another material, and its overall age-related wear.

Why this matters for a real estate transaction

Sewer lines aren't part of a typical home inspection in most cases, a general home inspector checks visible plumbing fixtures and accessible pipe, but the sewer lateral running underground from the house to the municipal connection or septic system is usually outside that scope. Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), doesn't require a camera inspection for a standard sale, which is exactly why this blind spot exists. For an older home, particularly one with original clay or cast iron piping, this is a real gap. A sewer line replacement or major repair after closing can be an expensive surprise that a pre-purchase camera inspection would have caught and let you negotiate around.

The report from a proper inspection typically documents the pipe material, its approximate age based on construction style, and any defects found with their distance from the access point. The result is an overall condition assessment, giving both buyer and seller a documented basis for negotiation rather than a vague verbal impression.

This is worth building into your timeline if you're under contract on an older Boca Raton property. A camera inspection typically takes under an hour, but scheduling it, along with getting the resulting report back in time to act on it before your inspection contingency deadline, means it shouldn't be left until the last few days before closing.

Reading the footage: what a technician is actually looking for

Watching raw sewer camera footage without context can be confusing, the pipe interior looks similar in a lot of places, and it's not always obvious what counts as a defect versus normal wear. A trained eye is checking for specific signals. Standing water pooling in one spot rather than flowing through suggests a bellied section. A visible gap or shadow line where two pipe sections meet suggests a possible offset joint. Thin root hairs versus a dense root mass distinguishes early from advanced intrusion, and the general texture and color of the pipe wall shows whether heavy corrosion or scaling is present. This is why a narrated walkthrough from the technician, pausing on anything worth noting, is more useful than raw footage handed over with no explanation.

Why this matters for a recurring drain problem

If a drain keeps clogging despite repeated snaking, a camera inspection answers the question that snaking alone can't. Is this grease and debris buildup that hydro jetting would resolve, or is it a structural issue, root intrusion, a bellied section, a crack, that needs actual repair rather than cleaning? Fixing the wrong problem, cleaning a structurally damaged line repeatedly instead of repairing it, wastes money and delays the real fix.

What it costs and how long it takes

Most residential inspections take under an hour, depending on the line's length and how many access points are available. Cost varies based on line length, whether a new access point needs to be created, and whether you're requesting a formal written report with footage for a real estate transaction versus a quick diagnostic look for a repair decision. Either way, it's a small cost relative to the alternative of guessing at a repair scope or discovering a major sewer issue after a home purchase closes.

For a repair decision specifically, we can often provide a rough estimate based on what the footage shows before we even leave the property, though a firm number usually follows once we confirm access to the specific section that needs work.

Ready to see what's actually going on?

Our sewer camera inspection service covers both real estate and diagnostic scenarios, with recorded footage provided either way. If the inspection finds root intrusion or grease buildup rather than a structural issue, hydro jetting may solve it without any digging. This is a common request from buyers looking at older homes in Highland Beach's condo buildings, where shared building laterals add another layer worth understanding before a purchase.

Whichever situation brought you here, the goal is the same: know what you're actually dealing with before spending money on a fix, rather than guessing and hoping.
