Septic vs. Sewer in Palm Beach County

By the Boca Raton 24/7 Plumbing team · September 17, 2026

If you're buying a home, or you've just moved to the area and someone mentioned septic tanks, it's worth understanding how sewer and septic actually break down across Palm Beach County, and specifically within Boca Raton. The short version: Boca Raton proper is overwhelmingly on municipal sewer, but that's not universally true across the wider county, and knowing which system a specific property has matters for how you maintain it going forward, well before any problem shows up.

How the two systems actually differ

Municipal sewer connects your home's wastewater line to a shared system that carries everything to a centralized treatment facility, maintained by the city or county utility. You pay a sewer fee, typically alongside your water bill, and beyond your own household plumbing, you're not responsible for maintaining the infrastructure that treats the waste. Septic is entirely different: it's a self-contained system on your own property, a tank that separates solids from liquids, and a drain field that disperses treated wastewater into the surrounding soil. There's no monthly sewer fee, but you're fully responsible for the system's maintenance, including periodic pumping.

Why Boca Raton specifically leans sewer

Boca Raton is a well-developed city, and its municipal sewer infrastructure has been extended throughout most of the city's built-up areas over decades of development. Septic systems in this area are the exception, mostly limited to a small number of older properties that predate a sewer line's extension to their street, or pockets at the city's edges that fall under different county jurisdiction. If you're buying a typical property within Boca Raton's city limits, sewer is the reasonable default assumption, though it's still worth confirming rather than assuming.

What to check before you assume either system

  • • Check your monthly utility bill; a sewer fee line item confirms municipal sewer service.
  • • Look for a septic tank access lid or drain field area in your yard, usually a rectangular section with slightly different grass growth.
  • • Contact the Palm Beach County Health Department, which maintains septic system permit records for the county.
  • • Check your property's title or closing documents from when you purchased, which sometimes note the system type.

What each system means for ongoing maintenance

Sewer-connected properties still need normal plumbing maintenance, but the wastewater treatment itself isn't your responsibility once it leaves your property line. Septic properties need active maintenance beyond typical household plumbing. That includes regular pumping every 3-5 years and periodic drain field inspection to catch early signs of saturation before a full failure. A septic system is also more sensitive to certain chemicals and non-biodegradable materials going down the drain than a municipal sewer system is.

Neglecting septic maintenance has real consequences beyond a plumbing inconvenience: a failed drain field is a genuinely expensive repair, and continued neglect can create actual health and environmental concerns from untreated wastewater reaching groundwater or the surface.

What if you just bought a home and aren't sure?

This is more common than people expect, especially for buyers relocating from an area where septic is rare or nonexistent. If your closing documents didn't clearly specify, or you inherited the property without a clear paper trail, treating an unknown system as septic until confirmed otherwise is the safer default, since assuming sewer when you're actually on septic means skipping maintenance a system genuinely needs. A quick call to the Palm Beach County Health Department with your address can typically confirm this within a reasonable timeframe, and it's worth doing early rather than waiting for a problem to force the question.

Selling a home with septic: what buyers will ask

If you're selling a septic property, expect buyers or their inspectors to ask about pumping history, drain field condition, and system age. Keeping documentation of past pumping dates and any repairs makes this conversation far smoother than having no records at all. Some buyers, particularly those unfamiliar with septic systems, may also request a septic-specific inspection as part of their due diligence, which is a reasonable request given the system represents a genuine ongoing responsibility they're taking on.

A note on hybrid situations

Occasionally a property has a more complicated setup than a clean sewer-or-septic split, an older property that was originally on septic and later connected to sewer without the septic tank being properly decommissioned, for instance. An improperly abandoned septic tank can pose its own risks, including ground settling over an empty tank if it wasn't filled in correctly. If you suspect this might be your situation, based on an unusually old property history or conflicting information from different sources, it's worth having a professional confirm the actual current setup rather than assuming either system in isolation tells the whole story.

The regulatory difference worth knowing

Sewer connections and the plumbing that ties into them fall under Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), enforced through the standard municipal permitting process. Septic systems, by contrast, are regulated separately by the Florida Department of Health, with their own permitting and inspection standards specific to septic design and installation. This is a genuinely different regulatory path, which is part of why a plumber addressing a sewer line issue and someone servicing a septic system are often, though not always, working within different specialized knowledge areas.

Not sure which applies to your property?

If you're on municipal sewer and dealing with a backup or slow drains, sewer line repair and camera inspection are the relevant services. If you've confirmed you're on septic, our septic tank pumping page covers what regular maintenance actually involves. This question comes up often from newer residents in Deerfield Beach, where the mix of city and county-jurisdiction areas nearby can make it genuinely unclear which system applies without checking directly.

Whichever system you have, we're happy to help you confirm it if you're genuinely unsure, since giving advice based on the wrong assumption helps nobody.
