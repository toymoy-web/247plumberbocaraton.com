Do You Need a Permit to Repipe in Boca Raton?

By the Boca Raton 24/7 Plumbing team · September 17, 2026

If you're planning a whole-home repipe in Boca Raton, the permit question comes up almost immediately, usually right after the estimate. The short answer is to ask the City of Boca Raton Development Services Department, but the details of why, and what an inspection covers, are worth understanding before the work starts rather than after.

Why the permit question matters

A permit is how the city confirms new plumbing work before it's closed up behind drywall where nobody can easily check it later. Florida plumbing rules are based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Development Services can tell you what the inspection covers for your scope. Skipping this step isn't just a paperwork risk, it can raise questions at resale, when an unpermitted repipe shows up during a buyer's inspection.

Within city limits, plumbing permit applications go through the City of Boca Raton Development Services Department. The process generally involves submitting an application with the scope of work, scheduling inspections at the stages the city requires, then a final sign-off once everything is connected and tested. Ask your contractor which inspections apply to your scope and who schedules them.

Older homes: ask what happens if walls reveal surprises

Opening walls in an older home can reveal conditions nobody planned for, such as damaged pipe, old insulation or framing that needs repair. This isn't unique to repiping, any project that opens walls on an older home can find surprises. Ask your contractor how unexpected findings would be handled and priced before work starts.

If your home was built before 1980, it's worth asking about this possibility during the estimate rather than being surprised by it mid-project. A contractor who's done this work in Boca Raton before should be able to tell you what to expect and roughly how it would affect the timeline and cost if remediation turns out to be necessary.

What happens if you skip the permit

  • • Unpermitted work can be flagged during a future home sale, requiring retroactive permitting or removal of the work.
  • • Ask your insurance company how unpermitted work could affect a claim before you start.
  • • A contractor who suggests skipping the permit "to save time" is taking on liability that ultimately falls back on you as the homeowner.

None of this means the process has to be slow. A straightforward residential repipe with no complications typically moves through rough-in and final inspection within the same overall project timeline as the physical work itself, especially when the contractor pulling the permit does this regularly.

PEX or copper: does the permit process differ?

PEX and copper are both widely used for residential supply lines, so ask your contractor and the building department to confirm that your chosen material is approved for your project. The decision between them usually comes down to budget and personal preference. PEX costs less and resists the corrosion that eventually affects metal pipe, while copper has a longer track record and some homeowners prefer it for resale expectations, particularly where buyers sometimes ask specifically about pipe material.

What to ask a contractor before signing off

A few questions during the estimate can save real headaches later. Ask whether they're pulling the permit themselves or expecting you to, since a homeowner-pulled permit shifts liability in ways many people don't realize until something goes wrong. Ask how they typically handle the lead-and-asbestos possibility on an older home, a contractor who's done this before should have a real answer, not a shrug. And ask what happens to the timeline if remediation is needed, since "we'll figure it out when we get there" is a very different answer from a contractor who has a concrete plan.

It's also worth asking whether the quote includes drywall repair after the work is done. Repiping necessarily means opening walls, and a contractor's scope of work should be clear about whether patching and repainting are included or handled separately, since that's a real cost difference that sometimes gets glossed over in an initial estimate.

How long does the permit process take

The permit application itself is usually the smaller part of the schedule. Inspection timing is what drives how long a repipe takes, so ask the city or your contractor what to expect for your address and scope. Scheduling inspections promptly, rather than letting a crew sit idle, is one of the more common reasons a repipe runs longer than expected. A contractor who does this work regularly will build inspection timing into the project plan from the start.

The bottom line

Yes, plan on a permit for a whole-home repipe in Boca Raton, and the process is manageable when a contractor who does this regularly is handling it. Confirm the details with Development Services for your address and scope. Ask about older-home surprises during your estimate rather than after the first wall comes down.

If you're seeing the early signs that a repipe might be in your future, repeated pinhole leaks, persistently low water pressure, or discolored water, our repiping and whole-home pipe replacement page covers what the process looks like in more detail. Leak detection can confirm the scope of the problem before you commit to a full repipe.

Boca Raton Note

Where Boca Raton permits are handled

Inside city limits, permits are handled by the City of Boca Raton Development Services Department at 200 NW 2nd Avenue, phone (561) 393-7930. The city's published permit checklists, such as the one for a water heater changeout, list a plumbing application and state that contractors must be registered and up to date to pull permits. Owner-builders follow a separate submittal path.

A repipe is a larger scope than a changeout, so call Development Services with your address and scope to confirm what your project needs. If your property is in a neighboring town, confirm which municipality issues permits for your address first.
