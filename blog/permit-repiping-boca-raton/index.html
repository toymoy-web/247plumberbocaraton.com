Do I Need a Permit for Repiping in Boca Raton?

By the Boca Raton 24/7 Plumbing team · September 17, 2026

If you're planning a whole-home repipe in Boca Raton, the permit question comes up almost immediately, usually right after the estimate. The short answer is yes, nearly every repipe requires a permit, but the details of why, and what the inspection actually checks, are worth understanding before the work starts rather than after.

Why repiping needs a permit at all

A permit exists to confirm that new plumbing work meets Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), before it's closed up behind drywall where nobody can easily check it later. For a repipe specifically, the inspector is verifying pipe material, sizing, support spacing, and that the new system was pressure-tested before any walls were closed. Skipping this step isn't just a paperwork risk, it can create a real problem at resale, when an unpermitted repipe shows up during a buyer's inspection and either has to be corrected or disclosed as a defect.

The City of Boca Raton's Building Department handles plumbing permits within city limits. The process typically involves submitting a permit application with the scope of work, scheduling a rough-in inspection after the new lines are run but before walls are closed, then a final inspection once everything is connected and tested.

The older-home wrinkle specific to Boca Raton

Here's the detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard: on pre-1980s properties, the Boca Raton Building Department can require lead-pipe or asbestos remediation if either is discovered during permitted plumbing work. This isn't unique to repiping, any permitted plumbing work on an older home can trigger it, but a whole-home repipe is exactly the kind of project where walls and slabs get opened up enough for something like old asbestos-wrapped pipe insulation to actually be found.

If your home was built before 1980, it's worth asking about this possibility during the estimate rather than being surprised by it mid-project. A contractor who's done this work in Boca Raton before should be able to tell you what to expect and roughly how it would affect the timeline and cost if remediation turns out to be necessary.

What happens if you skip the permit

  • • Unpermitted work can be flagged during a future home sale, requiring retroactive permitting or removal of the work.
  • • Insurance claims related to plumbing failures can be denied if the work wasn't permitted and inspected.
  • • A contractor who suggests skipping the permit "to save time" is taking on liability that ultimately falls back on you as the homeowner.

None of this means the process has to be slow. A straightforward residential repipe with no complications typically moves through rough-in and final inspection within the same overall project timeline as the physical work itself, especially when the contractor pulling the permit does this regularly.

PEX or copper: does the permit process differ?

Florida's plumbing code approves both PEX and copper for residential supply lines, so the permit and inspection process is essentially the same regardless of which material you choose. The decision between them usually comes down to budget and personal preference rather than anything code-related. PEX costs less and resists the corrosion that eventually affects metal pipe; copper has a longer track record and some homeowners prefer it for resale expectations, particularly in higher-end Boca Raton neighborhoods where buyers sometimes ask specifically about pipe material.

What to ask a contractor before signing off

A few questions during the estimate can save real headaches later. Ask whether they're pulling the permit themselves or expecting you to, since a homeowner-pulled permit shifts liability in ways many people don't realize until something goes wrong. Ask how they typically handle the lead-and-asbestos possibility on an older home, a contractor who's done this before should have a real answer, not a shrug. And ask what happens to the timeline if remediation is needed, since "we'll figure it out when we get there" is a very different answer from a contractor who has a concrete plan.

It's also worth asking whether the quote includes drywall repair after the work is done. Repiping necessarily means opening walls, and a contractor's scope of work should be clear about whether patching and repainting are included or handled separately, since that's a real cost difference that sometimes gets glossed over in an initial estimate.

How long does the permit process actually take

In practice, the permit application itself is usually the fastest part. Palm Beach County and the City of Boca Raton both process routine residential plumbing permits within a matter of business days for straightforward scopes of work, though this can vary with current permit office volume. The rough-in inspection happens once new lines are run but before drywall goes back up. Scheduling that inspection promptly, rather than letting a contractor's crew sit idle waiting on the city, is one of the more common reasons a repipe project runs longer than expected. A contractor who does this work regularly usually has a good sense of typical inspection turnaround and builds it into the project timeline from the start.

The bottom line

Yes, you need a permit for a whole-home repipe in Boca Raton, and yes, the process is manageable when a contractor who does this regularly is handling it. The one thing worth flagging early, especially on a pre-1980s home, is the possibility of lead or asbestos remediation being required if either turns up once walls are opened. Ask about it during your estimate rather than after the first wall comes down.
