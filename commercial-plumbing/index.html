What's Different Here

Commercial calls aren't just bigger residential jobs

Grease trap maintenance, backflow prevention testing, and multi-fixture restrooms are the plumbing realities of a commercial property, and each can come with its own local rules that a residential-focused visit wouldn't cover. Florida plumbing rules are based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and your city or county can tell you which backflow and grease interceptor requirements apply to your property.

Property managers and business owners also need scheduling flexibility that residential customers usually don't: after-hours work to avoid disrupting a retail floor during business hours, or emergency response for a restaurant that can't operate without working plumbing during peak hours.

Boca Raton's mix of older strip retail centers along Federal Highway and newer Class A office space near Mizner Park and the Town Center corridor means we see a wide range of plumbing ages and configurations. That ranges from original 1980s fixture layouts to buildings on modern PEX systems installed within the last few years.

Compliance

Why commercial backflow and grease rules matter more here

A residential backflow issue is a homeowner's problem. A commercial backflow failure can contaminate a public water system, so ask the City of Boca Raton about its backflow testing requirements for your property. Grease interceptors matter for food service businesses, since an undersized or poorly maintained interceptor can send grease into the municipal sewer system.

We can also handle the documentation side of compliance, providing dated service records for backflow tests and grease trap pumping. That's what most municipalities and health inspectors expect to see on file, rather than paperwork you have to track down after the fact.

Commercial Leaks

Commercial leak detection

Commercial buildings add distance and complexity to leak detection. Apartment complexes, plazas, offices and resorts have long runs of pipe and many tenants, so a high bill or a wet parking lot does not point to a single spot.

We trace the leak with electronic and acoustic equipment and tell you where it sits, how large it is and what the repair will involve. For a large property, we can survey the system in stages.

Backflow

Backflow testing for Boca Raton businesses

The City of Boca Raton's Cross-Connection Control and Backflow Protection Policy says non-residential customers are responsible for hiring a Certified Backflow Prevention Assembly Tester to test and re-certify non-fire-line backflow preventers every year. The test is due within 30 days before or after the previous certification expires.

The policy also says installation must be done by a State-licensed plumbing contractor and repairs by a Certified Backflow Prevention Repair Technician. Fire line devices are handled by a licensed fire protection contractor under separate rules, so tell us which type you have.
