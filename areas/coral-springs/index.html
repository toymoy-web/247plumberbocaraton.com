Housing Era

Why mid-age plumbing drives the call list here

Coral Springs grew as a planned city, with most of its neighborhoods built out between the late 1960s and the 1990s. That timing matters because supply lines from that window are now 30 to 55 years old. Original copper can develop pinhole leaks, and some homes from roughly the late 1970s through the mid 1990s were plumbed with polybutylene, a material known for failing.

We confirm the pipe material before quoting anything, since the right answer for a 1972 copper system is different from a 1989 home that may carry polybutylene. Our whole-home repiping page explains what a replacement involves and how to tell when it beats repeated spot repairs.

Neighborhoods and Landmarks

Where we dispatch inside Coral Springs

Before You Call

What to check first

  • • Is the water pressure low at every fixture or only one? A whole-house drop can point to aging supply lines.
  • • Do you see green or white crust on exposed copper fittings or under sinks?
  • • Has your water bill climbed without a change in habits? That is a common sign of a hidden leak.
  • • Is the water heater dry at the base and sitting on a drain pan?
  • • Is one patch of floor warm, or do you hear running water when everything is off?
Common Calls

What Coral Springs residents call about most

Repiping questions and leak detection lead the list, followed by water heater repair and replacement on tanks that are 12 to 15 years old. Most homes are slab on grade, so a slab leak often announces itself as a warm floor patch or a water meter that keeps moving.

Condo and townhome communities add another layer, since a leak in one unit can reach the unit below. Drain cleaning for shared stacks and camera inspection for repeat backups are regular requests from property managers. Call (561) 448-8545 and describe what you see.

Permits and Jurisdiction

Broward County rules apply here

Coral Springs is in Broward County, not Palm Beach County, so permit and inspection questions go to the local Broward building departments. That is one practical difference from our Boca Raton work, so we confirm the address on the first call.

Florida plumbing rules are based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), so ask the building department what applies to your project.

Water and Drainage

Lakes, a high water table, and hard water

Coral Springs is laid out around lakes and canals, and the water table sits close to the surface for much of the year. After heavy rain, that can show up as slow drains, backups at low points in a line, and soggy yard drains. Hard water is also common across South Florida, and it leaves scale on fixtures and shortens water heater life.

If a drain backs up after a storm, stop using the affected fixtures and call (561) 448-8545. We can run a camera through the line to see whether roots, a collapsed section, or a blocked cleanout is the cause. Our sewer camera inspection page shows what the footage reveals and how it guides the repair.
