Three ZIP Tiers

East, middle, and far west are three different plumbing ages

Emergency dispatch runs around the clock across every tier, from the original east side to the far-west gated communities.

East side (33426, 33435)

The city's original core near the Boynton Beach Inlet, with the oldest housing stock and the highest share of original supply lines.

Middle Boynton (33436, 33437)

Anchored by communities like Renaissance Commons and Montreux, largely built during the city's big 1970s growth wave.

Far west (33472, 33473)

Newer gated communities, generally on modern PEX or copper installed well within current code.

Knowing which tier a property falls into tells us a lot before we even arrive, since a 1970s middle-Boynton home and a newer far-west build call for very different starting assumptions about pipe material and likely failure points.

The 1970s Wave

Why so much of Boynton Beach is the same age

Boynton Beach's population grew faster in the 1970s than in almost any other decade in its history, and most homes in the 33436 area were built during that boom. That means a large share of the city's supply lines are now well past 45 years old, the range where copper pinhole leaks and galvanized restriction both become common. That's a real driver behind how often we're out doing leak detection and slab leak work in this specific ring of the city, more than citywide.

Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), governs any repipe or major repair the same way regardless of the home's age. A 1970s-era home is more likely to trigger permit-level work, simply because more of its original system needs replacing at once.

Far-west communities in 33472 and 33473 call mostly for routine water heater repair and drain cleaning, since their plumbing is newer and less prone to the aging-infrastructure issues seen closer to the coast.

The area around the Boynton Beach Inlet, part of the original east-side core, sees a disproportionate share of our older-home calls. It was among the first parts of the city built out after incorporation in 1920, and it carries the longest plumbing history of any ZIP we cover here.

Renaissance Commons and the newer mixed-use development around it sit right at the boundary between the older middle-Boynton tier and more recently built construction, so we treat that pocket case by case rather than assuming it fits either tier automatically.
