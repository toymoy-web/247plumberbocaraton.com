Housing Era

Why condo risers dominate the call list here

Highland Beach's building boom started in earnest in the 1970s and 80s, with communities like Villa Nova, Villa Magna, and the Boca Highlands Beach Club and Marina among the earliest large developments. That means a meaningful share of the town's plumbing is original vertical riser piping running through concrete-and-steel high-rises, not the slab-on-grade single-family plumbing common a few miles inland.

A leak or slow drain in a Highland Beach condo can point to a problem in your unit, or it can be a shared riser issue affecting multiple floors, which changes who's responsible and how the repair gets coordinated with the HOA or building management. We work both angles: diagnosing what's actually yours to fix versus what needs to go through the association.

Florida's plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), governs multi-family riser work the same way it governs single-family homes. A riser repair in an occupied high-rise also has to account for shutting off water to every unit on that stack, not just one household, which is part of why scheduling matters more here than in a standalone house.

What This Means for Repairs

Saltwater air and shared plumbing systems

Emergency dispatch runs around the clock for Highland Beach buildings, since a shared riser issue affecting multiple floors can't wait for business hours any more than a burst pipe in a single-family home can.

Faster corrosion

Constant salt air accelerates corrosion on copper supply lines and fixtures compared to inland homes, which is part of why leak detection calls run higher here.

Shared risers

A backup or leak can originate in a neighboring unit's line, which is why we check the riser before assuming the problem is isolated to your unit.

HOA coordination

Work on shared lines often needs sign-off from the building or HOA, which we factor into scheduling from the first call.

Common Calls

What Highland Beach residents call about most

Given the concentration of high-rise condos, water heater repair for smaller in-unit tank and tankless units, toilet repair, and leak detection for suspected riser issues are the most common calls. That's ahead of the slab leak and sewer line work that's more typical a few miles west in single-family neighborhoods. Water damage restoration also comes up after a unit-to-unit leak, since the cleanup for a downstairs neighbor's ceiling is a real and recurring need in stacked construction.

We also field a steady number of calls from part-time and seasonal owners whose units sit empty for months at a time, since a slow supply-line leak can run undetected far longer in an unoccupied unit than in a home someone checks daily. If you split time between Highland Beach and elsewhere, it's worth having a shutoff valve check done during your visits, not just when something's already gone wrong.
