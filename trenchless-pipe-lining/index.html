Trenchless Pipe Lining in Boca Raton, FL

If a camera inspection found multiple weak points along a sewer or drain line, but the pipe itself is otherwise intact, lining it from the inside can fix the entire run without digging up a yard, driveway, or landscaping to reach it.

What This Actually Is

A new pipe inside the old one

Most people calling about this describe wanting to avoid digging up their yard, or they've been quoted a full excavation and are looking for an alternative. Trenchless lining works by inserting a resin-saturated liner into the existing pipe, then curing it in place to form a smooth, jointless new pipe within the old one's shell. The result is a structurally sound line without trenching across a driveway, mature landscaping, or a pool deck to reach it.

It's worth noting this method works on drain and sewer lines specifically. Pressurized supply lines carrying potable water use a related but distinct technique, so the two shouldn't be assumed interchangeable when comparing quotes.

Whether lining needs a permit depends on the local building department, so ask the City of Boca Raton Development Services Department before work starts. Florida plumbing rules are based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a contractor should be able to tell you what applies at your address.

Boca Raton's mature landscaping and hardscaped properties, especially in the older east-side neighborhoods, are exactly the situation lining was designed for. A homeowner facing a sewer line repair usually doesn't want an excavator tearing through decades-old oak trees or a recently installed paver driveway to reach a line that's mostly still sound.

The Process

What actually happens during the job

The line is first cleaned and camera-inspected to confirm it's a good candidate. A flexible liner soaked in resin is then inserted through an existing access point or a small new one, inflated to press against the pipe walls, and cured in place, typically using heat, UV light, or ambient curing depending on the resin system. The result is a smooth new pipe surface bonded to the inside of the old one, usually completed within a single day for most residential lines.

A follow-up camera pass confirms the cured liner is fully seated and the line is flowing correctly before we consider the job finished, rather than assuming a successful cure based on time and temperature readings alone. We provide that final footage as part of the completed job record.
